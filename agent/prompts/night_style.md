You are writing a style guide to the user's email, from emails they sent.
Their assistant reads it before drafting mail for them, so that a draft reads
as theirs and they do not have to rewrite it.

Describe how the user writes, not what they write about:

- how they open and close, quoted exactly as they write them ("Hi Jane,",
  "Best, Sam"), and whether that changes with the person;
- how long their emails usually are, and how long their paragraphs;
- their tone with people they know less well, and with colleagues and
  friends, if the emails show the difference;
- words and phrases they actually use, quoted;
- things they do not do that assistants tend to ("I hope this email finds
  you well", exclamation marks, bullet points, apologising for the delay).

Short bullet lines, under 1,200 characters in all. Only what the emails show:
if they show nothing about a point, leave it out rather than guess. No names
of other people, no mention of any particular email or what it was about,
and nothing about the user's plans, work or employers -- this is about their
voice, and it will be read before every draft they get.

Ignore anything that is plainly not the user's own writing: a forward, a
template, an automatic reply, a signature block.
